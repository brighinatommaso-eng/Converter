# v0.1.5 - PDF to Word: editable and laid out like the original

Converter 0.1.5 rebuilds PDF to Word conversion. A local AI model reads the page
layout, and the Word file is both editable and laid out like the original. This
release also fixes bugs that silently dropped text from many PDFs.

## PDF → Word: one mode, editable *and* faithful

The old choice between "Editable structure" and "Visual layout" is gone. There is
now one Word file that does both, with Word's own tools:

- **Pages:** one section per PDF page, with the page size and margins of the original.
- **Columns:** text in two or three columns stays in columns. Columns are an invisible
  table, so they stay put in Word and LibreOffice.
- **Spacing and indents:** line spacing, the space between blocks and indents are
  measured on the PDF, so the lines land at the same height.
- **Headings:** they look like the original (font, size, colour) but are still Word
  headings, so the navigation pane and the table of contents work.
- **Lists and tables:** lists are real Word lists. Tables keep their column widths,
  row heights and cell colours.
- **Coloured boxes and backgrounds:** coloured boxes become paragraph shading.
  Full-page photos sit behind the text.
- **Images and charts:** they stay where they were. An image with text beside it is
  anchored to its position.
- **Headers and footers:** they become Word headers and footers, and the page number
  is a real page-number field.
- **Fonts:** when the original font is not installed, Converter picks a standard one
  of the same kind (Times New Roman, Arial or Courier New) and narrows it slightly.
  Lines then break where they did in the original.

## The AI layout model

- **What it does:** a local model (PP-DocLayoutV2, Apache-2.0) looks at each rendered
  page and finds titles, paragraphs, tables, figures and formulas. It also reads the
  order of multi-column layouts.
- **Where the words come from:** always from the PDF. The model only decides where
  the blocks are.
- **Privacy:** it runs offline on the CPU. Nothing leaves your computer.
- **Charts and formulas:** they are kept as images, including vector charts that used
  to disappear.
- **Speed:** about 1–2 seconds per page. The progress bar shows the page being analysed.

## Text that used to disappear

- **Text inside "form objects"** is read now. This is common in InDesign layouts,
  annual reports and filled forms, and up to 97% of a page could go missing silently.
  On the test set, more than 1,900 words came back and none was lost.
- **Pages that are halves of a spread** no longer carry the invisible half of the
  other page.
- **Scanned PDFs with an OCR text layer** give the recognised, editable text in Word
  instead of the photo with the text drawn over it.
- **Images hidden in the PDF** (clipped away) no longer reappear in the output.

## Other changes

- The percentage on the progress bar is readable all the way: white over the filled
  part, dark over the empty part.
- The Windows build uses ffmpeg "essentials". Every codec the app uses is checked
  during the build.

## Honest about the limits

- **Exact page count:** on the 32-PDF test set, 24 files come out with exactly the
  same number of pages as the original. The others gain a page or two where the
  substitute font wraps differently. This happens mostly with Korean text and with
  very dense infographics.
- **Text over pictures:** text drawn over a picture that is not a full-page background
  is placed next to it, not over it.
- **Size:** the AI model makes the app larger. Windows installed: about 620 MB.

## Verification

- 446 automated tests pass on Linux with ffmpeg, LibreOffice and the AI model. The
  Windows suite runs without external tools.
- Every package is self-tested before release, including a check that the AI model
  loads and analyses a page.
